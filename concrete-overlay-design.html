Concrete Overlay Design

Part of: Concrete Pavement Overlays is-subtopic-of
Concrete Overlay Design is a topic under Concrete Pavement Overlays.
overlay-thicknessaashto-93pavement-medesign-inputsseparation-layer
Created 2026-09-10 · Updated 2026-09-12

Structural design of concrete overlays: thickness determination, material and support inputs (PCC properties, resilient modulus, load-transfer and drainage coefficients) and interlayer design. Overlay Thickness Calculation determines the required concrete depth to restore structural capacity, while PCC Material Properties and Structural Design defines the mix variables and thermal characteristics needed for that layer. [1][4] Geotextile Separation Layer Design specifies the interlayer fabric for unbonded applications to isolate the new slab from existing pavement movements. [1][5]

CP Tech Center Guides reports covering “Concrete Overlay Design” also include the following subtopics: Geotextile Separation Layer Design, Overlay Thickness Calculation, and PCC Material Properties and Structural Design.

Design inputs and requirements

Design inputs for concrete overlays are governed by the structural capacity requirements of the 1993 AASHTO Guide and the material performance criteria of AASHTOWare Pavement ME Design, which mandate specific traffic reliability targets and concrete strength parameters. [1][4] Furthermore, the design process integrates site-specific climatic data and thermal properties, including coefficient of thermal expansion, to ensure the selected materials can withstand environmental stresses and maintain long-term serviceability. [1]

The following figure illustrates the predicted punchout performance of an 8.0-inch unbonded CRCP overlay as determined by this design methodology.

AASHTOWare Pavement ME Design Predicted Punchouts Plot for 8.0 in. Unbonded CRCP Overlay. [1]

The figure displays a plot of predicted punchout rates per mile against pavement age, comparing performance at specified reliability levels to a defined threshold value.

Structural section dimensions

Structural section dimensions in concrete overlay design are primarily governed by the required slab thickness calculated via the 1993 AASHTO Guide, which is adjusted upward by approximately 0.5 inches when a nonwoven geotextile separation layer replaces traditional asphalt interlayers to maintain structural adequacy. [1][4][5] The Geotextile Separation Layer Design further dictates that fabric weight and thickness must be matched to the overlay depth, while aggregate subbase thickness can be reduced from eight inches to four or five inches when utilizing this separation barrier. [2][5] Additionally, PCC Material Properties and Structural Design imposes a minimum combined pavement and overlay thickness of six inches for mechanistic-empirical analysis, with joint spacing typically limited to 12–15 feet and dowel reinforcement specified at 1.25-inch diameters on 12-inch centers. [1][3]

Materials and mixture design

Concrete overlay materials are defined by specific mechanical and thermal properties, including a target modulus of elasticity of approximately 4,800,000 psi and a 28-day flexural strength between 650 and 680 psi. [1] Material characteristics are integrated with site-specific climatic data and groundwater conditions to ensure the overlay can withstand traffic loads and environmental stresses while maintaining structural integrity. [1]

The following figure illustrates the interface used to configure these structural parameters within the AASHTOWare Pavement ME Design software.

Screen Capture of AASHTOWare Pavement ME Design Pavement Structure/Layers Menu. [1]

Visible input fields allow the user to specify general properties such as thickness and unit weight, as well as thermal characteristics like the coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE). The source context explains that this menu is used to define material properties for concrete overlays, noting that CTE is a critical design variable.

Drainage and accessibility

Drainage in concrete overlays is managed by separation layers that either act as impervious barriers or channel infiltrating water along the cross-slope to pavement edges, where geotextile fabrics must extend at least four inches beyond the margin into drainable substrates or under-drain systems. [1][5] Accessibility and safety are ensured by maintaining these drainage outlets unobstructed for equipment movement while securing the separation layer with anchors or fasteners spaced no more than six feet apart to prevent wrinkling during construction. [2][6]

The following figure illustrates the specific detailing required for drainage outlets in geotextile or HMA separation layers.

Cross-sectional details of Unbonded Concrete Overlay on Concrete (UBCOC) widening units showing drainage outlets for separation layers. [6]

The figure displays two cross-sections of a UBCOC widening detail, illustrating the placement of an unbonded concrete overlay over existing pavement and subbase materials. It specifically depicts drainage outlets where the separation layer is daylighted to the ditch foreslope or connected to a working subdrain, utilizing either a nonwoven geotextile fabric or an HMA separation layer. This visualizes the interlayer design and drainage requirements necessary to prevent stripping in unbonded concrete overlays.
