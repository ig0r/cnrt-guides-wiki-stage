Pavement Foundations and Drainage

Part of: CP Tech Center Guides is-subtopic-of
Pavement Foundations and Drainage is a topic under CP Tech Center Guides.
subgradesubbasebasecompactionedge-drainsfoundation-layers
Created 2026-09-10 · Updated 2026-09-12

The support system beneath the concrete slab: subgrade, subbase and base layers, their compaction and stabilisation, and the subsurface drainage that keeps the foundation uniform, stiff and dry. Aggregate Subbase Performance and Pavement Structural Elements define the material composition, structural dimensions, and drainage requirements that establish a uniform foundation. [4][7][8] Pavement Substructure Compaction and Subgrade and Base Stabilization provide the densification methods and chemical treatments necessary to achieve the required stiffness and support. [3][6][9] Edge Drain Design and Installation supplies the specific subsurface drainage systems that remove excess water from joints and base layers to prevent moisture-related distresses. [8]

CP Tech Center Guides reports covering “Pavement Foundations and Drainage” also include the following subtopics: Subgrade and Base Stabilization, Aggregate Subbase Performance, Edge Drain Design and Installation, Pavement Structural Elements, and Pavement Substructure Compaction.

Design inputs and requirements

Design inputs and requirements for pavement foundations are driven by the need to provide uniform support, effective load distribution, and moisture control through site-specific characterization of subgrade stiffness, drainage coefficients, and traffic loading. [7] Material specifications mandate limits on particle size, fines content, plasticity, and abrasion resistance to ensure durability, while compaction criteria require achieving target densities or stiffness moduli to prevent settlement-related distresses such as cracking and pumping. [3][10]

The following figure illustrates pore pressure distribution within the aggregate base and subgrade to demonstrate moisture control mechanisms.

Diagram of pore pressure wave propagation in pavement layers under a moving load. [3]

The figure depicts a cross-section of a pavement structure consisting of a PCC Wearing Surface, an Aggregate Base layer, and the Subgrade beneath it. A wheel is shown moving in the direction of travel over the surface, generating a pore pressure wave that propagates horizontally through the aggregate base and vertically into the subgrade. This mechanism illustrates how water infiltration can lead to nonuniformity of support caused by differences in subgrade soil strength or moisture, a key factor in pavement foundation distress.

Materials and mixture design

Pavement foundations rely on a combination of aggregate subbases and edge-drain systems to provide uniform structural support while actively managing moisture infiltration. [2][7][8] Aggregate materials are selected for their gradation, durability, and permeability to distribute traffic loads and act as capillary breaks, whereas edge drains utilize open-graded aggregates and perforated collectors to remove trapped water from beneath the pavement structure. [3][7][8] Together, these components prevent settlement, pumping, and frost heave by ensuring a stable, well-drained interface between the subgrade and the pavement surface. [2][3][8]

The following figure illustrates how concrete pavement is affected by these surface and subsurface moisture conditions.

Concrete pavement exposed to surface and subsurface moisture conditions [3]

The figure illustrates a cross-section of a concrete road slab where blue arrows indicate water infiltration from rain clouds into the joints and surrounding soil. A rectangular zone beneath the pavement is labeled as being exposed to constant high moisture levels, highlighting the interaction between surface runoff, subsurface flow, and capillary fringe from the water table. This visualizes the mechanism of moisture accumulation in the foundation layers that necessitates drainage systems to prevent settlement and frost heave.

Drainage and accessibility

Effective drainage and accessibility in pavement foundations rely on the integration of aggregate subbases, edge drains, and stabilized layers to manage moisture infiltration and ensure structural uniformity. [2][7][8] Aggregate subbases provide a highly permeable, stable platform that isolates the pavement from subsurface moisture while facilitating rapid water removal through targeted subdrain systems. [7][8] Complementing this, edge drain designs intercept excess water at the pavement edges, preventing trapped moisture from causing distresses such as pumping and faulting, maintaining long-term accessibility and serviceability. [2][8]

The figure below illustrates how adding a longitudinal edge drain improves the drainability of the granular base layer.

Schematic of a longitudinal edge drain installation reducing the flow path within a granular base layer. [2]

The figure displays a cross-section of pavement layers, including a surface and a granular base containing water droplets. It illustrates the ‘New shortened flow path’ where water is intercepted by a perforated pipe at the edge of the base layer, rather than flowing horizontally to an outlet.

Quality control and acceptance

Quality control and acceptance for pavement foundations center on verifying that subgrade compaction achieves uniform stiffness to prevent settlement, while aggregate subbases are validated against gradation, density, and permeability thresholds to ensure stable load distribution. [3][7]

The figure below illustrates a permeability test used to validate these drainage characteristics.

Field permeability test on pavement support layers using a double-ring infiltrometer. [7]

The image shows workers conducting an in situ field test where water is poured into a metal ring device placed directly on the ground surface to measure infiltration rates.
