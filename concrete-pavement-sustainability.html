Concrete Pavement Sustainability

Part of: CP Tech Center Guides is-subtopic-of
Concrete Pavement Sustainability is a topic under CP Tech Center Guides.
sustainabilitylccalcarecyclingrcapervious-concrete
Created 2026-09-10 · Updated 2026-09-12

Economic and environmental evaluation of concrete pavement choices over the life cycle: life-cycle cost analysis, life-cycle assessment, long-life and low-impact construction practices, recycling of concrete pavements, and stormwater-managing pervious concrete. Concrete Pavement Recycling and Sustainability supplies the material recycling and reuse framework, while Life-Cycle Assessment of Environmental Impact provides the quantitative evaluation methods and tools for environmental impact mitigation. [1][2][3] Sustainable Concrete Pavement Construction establishes the sustainable construction practices for long-life systems, and Pervious Concrete Stormwater Management applies these principles to stormwater management through permeable surfacing. [1][3][4] Life-Cycle Assessment of Environmental Impact and Life-Cycle Cost Analysis supply the quantitative evaluation methods and tools required to measure environmental burdens and economic worth over the service life. [2][3]

CP Tech Center Guides reports covering “Concrete Pavement Sustainability” also include the following subtopics: Concrete Pavement Recycling and Sustainability, Life-Cycle Assessment of Environmental Impact, Life-Cycle Cost Analysis, Pervious Concrete Stormwater Management, and Sustainable Concrete Pavement Construction.

Evaluation methods and tools

Quantitative tools are often supplemented by structured rating systems such as Greenroads and INVEST, which provide standardized criteria for self-evaluation and certification of sustainability best practices during planning, design, construction, and maintenance phases. [1][3]

Sustainable construction practices

Sustainable construction practices for concrete pavement prioritize a zero-waste lifecycle by integrating low-energy plant operations, local material sourcing, and in-place recycling to minimize emissions and resource extraction. [1][4] Methods are complemented by the use of pervious concrete systems for stormwater management and long-life designs that reduce maintenance frequency through durable, high-reflectivity surfaces. [1][3][6]

Diagram illustrating the sustainability features of concrete pavement systems. [1]

The figure displays a cutaway view of a layered concrete pavement structure annotated with various environmental and performance benefits, including improved fuel economy, lower energy footprint, and industrial by-product use.

Material recycling and reuse

Material recycling and reuse in concrete pavement sustainability is driven by a zero-waste goal that prioritizes the complete recovery of end-of-life pavements into recycled concrete aggregate for new construction or base layers. [1][3] The approach minimizes reliance on virgin aggregates, reduces transportation emissions through local sourcing and in-place processing, and supports broader environmental objectives such as carbon sequestration via accelerated carbonation. [1][4] Success is measured by systematic life-cycle assessments that quantify reductions in embodied energy, landfill diversion, and overall lifecycle costs while maintaining structural performance. [1][2][5]

Environmental impact mitigation

Environmental impact mitigation in concrete pavement sustainability is achieved by prioritizing zero-waste strategies that completely recycle end-of-life materials into recycled concrete aggregate, minimizing the extraction of virgin resources and landfill disposal. [1][3] The approach is complemented by life-cycle assessment tools that quantify and optimize environmental burdens across all stages, from raw material extraction to construction and maintenance, ensuring that decisions are driven primarily by sustainability goals rather than short-term economic factors. [1][2][3]
