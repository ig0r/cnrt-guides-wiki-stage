Pavement Repairs

Part of: Pavement Repair is-subtopic-of
Pavement Repairs is a topic under Pavement Repair.
full-depth-repairspavement-rehabilitationrepair-materialsstructural-integrityconstruction-practices
Created 2026-09-01 · Updated 2026-09-02

Pavement repairs are maintenance interventions designed to restore the structural integrity, load-bearing capacity, and surface functionality of concrete roadways compromised by distress such as cracking, spalling, or joint deterioration. These interventions are fundamentally classified by the depth of damage relative to the slab thickness, distinguishing between partial-depth repairs for near-surface defects and full-depth replacements for structural failures that extend through the entire pavement layer [2] [3] [4]. The repair process involves removing unsound material, preparing the substrate to ensure proper bonding or separation, and placing compatible concrete mixes often reinforced with dowels or steel to reestablish load transfer across joints [1] [3] [4]. Final surface finishing operations, such as diamond grinding, are applied to correct roughness and ensure a smooth riding surface that meets safety and serviceability standards [2] [3].

Key findings

  • Evaluated the depth of deterioration to determine whether partial-depth or full-depth repairs were required, finding that damage extending less than half the slab thickness warranted partial-depth repair while deeper damage necessitated full-depth intervention [1] [2].
  • Demonstrated that partial-depth repairs provided a service life of 10 to 20 years when executed with durable materials and sound construction practices, but noted that poor workmanship or materials could reduce this lifespan to just 2 to 3 years [1] [2].
  • Identified that partial-depth repairs were unsuitable for distresses involving dowel-bar lockup, mechanical cracking, or failure extending deeper than half the slab thickness [1].
  • Showed that an unbonded concrete overlay served as an effective major rehabilitation technique for pavements with high-severity spalling, eliminating reflective cracking through the use of a separation layer and allowing milled joints to be patched [2].

  • Established that consistent quality assurance, including proper curing to prevent shrinkage cracking and adherence to specific material standards such as low water-cementitious ratios or rapid-setting cements, was essential for long-lasting repairs [1].

Concrete Pavement Repair Methods

Concrete pavement repair involves restoring structural integrity and surface functionality to roadways compromised by distress such as cracking, spalling, or joint deterioration [2]. Repair methods are primarily classified by the depth of damage relative to slab thickness, distinguishing between partial-depth treatments for near-surface issues and full-depth interventions that replace material down to the base [1] [3] [4]. Effective restoration requires removing unsound concrete, preparing the substrate to ensure proper bonding or separation, and installing compatible materials often reinforced with load-transfer devices like dowels to maintain slab continuity [2] [3] [4].

Joint board installation in a concrete pavement repair. [2]

The image displays a longitudinal section of a concrete pavement where a rectangular void has been excavated to expose the underlying layers. A brown, corrugated joint board is positioned vertically within this trench, separating the existing slab from the fresh material being placed in the foreground. This component serves as a resilient filler to re-establish the joint or crack by preventing the new concrete from bonding to the old slab, which is a critical step in pavement repairs for restoring structural integrity.

Research problem — Premature joint deterioration, particularly spalling that allows water and de-icing chemicals to penetrate underlying layers, creates a critical need for timely assessment to determine whether partial-depth or full-depth repairs are required [2]. Uncontrolled cracking and punchouts undermine structural performance and safety, necessitating specific remedial actions such as full-depth patching to restore integrity when damage exceeds the capacity of shallower repair methods [3]. Achieving durable repairs in airport environments is challenged by difficulties in maintaining quality for fixed-form placements, adverse weather effects on concrete properties, and edge spalling caused by improper sawing operations [4].

The following figure illustrates the severe joint spalling that necessitates such timely assessment and remedial actions.

Concrete pavement with severe joint spalling (photo courtesy of Todd LaTorella, MO/KS ACPA) [2]

The image displays a concrete roadway surface characterized by extensive deterioration along the longitudinal joints. Visible damage includes severe joint spalling, where chunks of concrete have broken away from the edges, creating rough and uneven surfaces.

State of the art — Current practice employs a condition-based framework that matches observed distress severity to either partial-depth or full-depth interventions, with selection guided by decision matrices and established specifications [1] [2] [4]. Partial-depth repairs are the preferred solution for localized defects confined to the upper half of the slab, utilizing rapid-setting materials and precise boundary marking to achieve service lives of ten to twenty years [1] [2] [4]. Full-depth repairs are required for severe structural distresses such as corner breaks or deep spalling, involving complete slab replacement with engineered load-transfer devices and strict quality control to restore long-term performance [1] [2] [3] [4].

Novelty & rigor — The research introduces a structured decision-matrix framework that links observed joint deterioration causes and rates to specific preservation or rehabilitation actions, replacing ad-hoc judgment with an asset-management approach for optimal timing and cost [2]. Distinctive innovations include a partial-depth repair method that utilizes V- or round-shaped milling heads to create tapered edges for improved bond strength, alongside the systematic use of rapid-strength binders like calcium sulfoaluminate cement to ensure durability [1]. The methodology is uniquely characterized by a single full-depth saw cut combined with drilled-hole tie bars for structural continuity, which significantly reduces labor and lane-closure durations compared to traditional multi-cut techniques [1].

Across the reviewed reports, repair selection for concrete pavements is primarily determined by the depth of distress relative to slab thickness, with partial-depth methods reserved for upper-half damage and full-depth repairs required when deterioration exceeds half the slab or involves structural failure mechanisms. While partial-depth repairs offer a cost-effective solution for shallow spalling and surface defects with service lives ranging from two to twenty years depending on material quality, full-depth interventions are necessary for deeper structural issues, working cracks, or high-severity joint deterioration. The durability of both repair types relies heavily on precise boundary definition through coring or testing and strict adherence to construction protocols, including proper curing and material selection, to prevent premature failure due to shrinkage or poor workmanship. Implications — Agencies must prioritize rapid, data-driven diagnosis to classify distress by depth and activity, ensuring that repair strategies are matched precisely to the severity of the failure [2]. Partial-depth repairs should be reserved for surface-level deterioration confined to the upper slab half, requiring conservative sizing, high-quality low-shrinkage materials, and rigorous curing to prevent early failure [1]. Full-depth interventions are necessary for active cracks or extensive damage exceeding half the slab thickness, necessitating standardized designs with effective load-transfer systems to ensure long-term durability [2].

Limitations — Partial-depth repairs are restricted to shallow distress confined to the upper portion of the slab and are unsuitable for deep deterioration or specific macro-repetitive distress patterns [1] [2]. The durability of full-depth repairs is contingent upon proper load-transfer design and construction quality, with performance diminishing significantly if the pavement is already severely deteriorated or if extensive joint work is required [1] [2]. Material compatibility issues, such as poor freeze-thaw durability in harsh climates and risks of bond failure due to shrinkage or thermal expansion, constrain the long-term effectiveness of many repair mixes [1].
