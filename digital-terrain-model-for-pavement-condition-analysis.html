Digital Terrain Model for Pavement Condition Analysis

digital-terrain-model3d-engineeringinfrastructure-designpavement-condition-analysisgeotechnical-assessment
Created 2026-09-09 · Updated 2026-09-09

The Digital Terrain Model for Pavement Condition Analysis is an accurate, continuous three‑dimensional representation of the existing ground surface that serves as the foundational layer for a 3D design model[1]. It is produced by fusing topographic survey data captured with total stations or GPS, aerial LiDAR contour maps, and high‑density point clouds from mobile or terrestrial LiDAR scanners into a single surface file that can be visualized as a triangulated network or computer‑generated contours[1]. The resulting model depicts both surface topography and subsurface soil layers, forming a comprehensive 3D site model that can be combined with utility information to support pavement condition analysis[1].

Purpose and design objectives

The Digital Terrain Model is created to supply an accurate, continuous three‑dimensional representation of the existing ground surface so it can serve as the foundational layer for a 3D design model. By integrating aerial LiDAR contours and raw survey shots into a single surface file, engineers obtain a reliable terrain base that supports visualization, analysis, and subsequent infrastructure design work. [1]

Design methods and criteria

The Digital Terrain Model for Pavement Condition Analysis is constructed through an integrated set of surveying and data‑fusion procedures. Ground‑surface information is first captured by topographic surveys using total stations or GPS (“topographic survey data obtained by total stations or global positioning systems”) and by aerial LiDAR contour maps, while additional high‑density point clouds are gathered with mobile or terrestrial LiDAR scanners (“point clouds obtained by mobile or terrestrial LiDAR”). Raw survey shots are also incorporated. All of these datasets are merged into a single surface file, producing a continuous terrain representation that can be visualized either as a triangulated network linking the points or as computer‑generated contours draped over the model (“Compiling the data from these sources into one surface file yields a continuous surface that can be visualized with triangles between the data points or with computer‑generated contours draped over the whole surface”). The resulting digital terrain model depicts both the surface topography and subsurface soil layers, forming a comprehensive 3D model of the existing site (“comprehensive 3D model of the existing site”) that can be combined with subsurface utility information to serve as the base map for pavement condition analysis. [1]
