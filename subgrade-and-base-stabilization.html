Subgrade and Base Stabilization

Part of: Pavement Foundations and Drainage is-subtopic-of
Subgrade and Base Stabilization is a topic under Pavement Foundations and Drainage.
cement-stabilizationcement-modified-soilfull-depth-reclamationsoil-cementadditive-testing
Created 2026-09-10 · Updated 2026-09-12

Chemical treatment of foundation soils and reclaimed pavement layers with cement or other additives to produce a bound or improved platform: mix composition, field process, full-depth reclamation, compaction control and the laboratory testing that verifies the treated material. Cement-Modified Soils Subgrade Improvement and Cement Stabilization Process supply the field methods that convert unbound soils into bound platforms, with the latter specifying the dry-spreading and compaction sequence required for full stabilization. [2][4] The resulting material properties are defined by Cement-Stabilized Subgrade Composition, which establishes the lean mix limits and gradation constraints necessary to create a cohesive foundation beneath pavement layers. [3][4] Engineers verify these improvements through Soil Compaction and Additive Testing, which quantifies volumetric swell and unconfined compressive strength to confirm that the treated subgrade meets durability and bearing capacity requirements. [1]

CP Tech Center Guides reports covering “Subgrade and Base Stabilization” also include the following subtopics: Cement Stabilization Process, Cement-Modified Soils Subgrade Improvement, Cement-Stabilized Subgrade Composition, and Soil Compaction and Additive Testing.

Material composition and properties

Subgrade stabilization materials are composite layers formed by blending native granular soils or recycled concrete aggregate with cementitious binders such as Portland cement, lime, or fly ash to create a bound, load-bearing foundation. [3][5] The resulting material properties are governed by gradation limits, low fines content, and specific soil classifications that ensure mechanical interlock and chemical stability while mitigating moisture-induced volumetric changes. [1][4][6]

Field construction processes

Field construction for subgrade stabilization begins with preparing the work area by shaping and correcting unstable zones, followed by an initial pulverization pass to create a uniform surface before cement is distributed using mechanical spreaders. [1][2] The material is then blended in-place or plant-mixed to achieve homogeneity, compacted promptly to specified density requirements, and finished to the final grade. [1][2] Finally, the stabilized layer undergoes a mandatory curing period of at least 24 hours while kept continuously moist to ensure proper hydration before further construction activities resume. [1][2]

Quality control and testing

Quality control for subgrade stabilization relies on verifying that material composition meets gradation and plasticity limits while ensuring the final layer achieves specified density through standard Proctor testing supplemented by Dynamic Cone Penetrometer assessments. [1][2] Performance validation further requires laboratory evaluation of volumetric swell and unconfined compressive strength to confirm that treated soils retain adequate bearing capacity after water exposure, guaranteeing long-term durability against moisture-induced deterioration. [1]
