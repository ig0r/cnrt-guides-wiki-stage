Concrete Pavement Preservation and Rehabilitation

Part of: CP Tech Center Guides is-subtopic-of
Concrete Pavement Preservation and Rehabilitation is a topic under CP Tech Center Guides.
preservationcprfull-depth-repairpartial-depth-repairslab-stabilizationresealing
Created 2026-09-10 · Updated 2026-09-12

Treatments applied to existing concrete pavements, from non-structural preservation (grinding, resealing, drainage retrofit) through partial- and full-depth repair and slab stabilisation, and the strategy for selecting among them. Concrete Pavement Preservation Practices provide the non-structural interventions that maintain serviceability and arrest early-stage deterioration, while Partial-Depth Repair Techniques address near-surface failures confined to the upper slab layers. [1][5][6] Full-Depth Pavement Repair Method restores structural capacity when distress penetrates substantially through the slab, and Grout Injection for Slab Stabilization corrects isolated depressions caused by underlying voids in structurally intact slabs. [1][5][9] Partial-Depth Repair (PDR) Techniques target near-surface distress confined to the upper slab layers, restoring continuity without full replacement, whereas Grout Injection for Slab Stabilization corrects isolated depressions caused by voids beneath structurally intact slabs. [5][7][8]

CP Tech Center Guides reports covering “Concrete Pavement Preservation and Rehabilitation” also include the following subtopics: Concrete Pavement Preservation Practices, Full-Depth Pavement Repair Method, Grout Injection for Slab Stabilization, and Partial-Depth Repair (PDR) Techniques.

Distress characterization and causes

Concrete pavement distress originates from a combination of age-related material loss, traffic loading, and moisture intrusion, manifesting as surface defects like spalling, cracking, faulting, and D-cracking that degrade ride quality and structural integrity. [1][6] The severity of these conditions dictates the appropriate intervention: early-stage deterioration is addressed through preservation practices aimed at arresting decay, while deeper damage penetrating beyond one-third to one-half of the slab thickness necessitates partial- or full-depth repairs to restore load transfer and structural capacity. [1][5][6]

Applicability and treatment selection

Applicability and treatment selection for concrete pavement preservation hinge on the Pavement Condition Rating (PCR), with preventive maintenance reserved for pavements in good condition (PCR 60–100) to arrest early-stage deterioration, while lower ratings necessitate structural rehabilitation or reconstruction. [1][6] Treatment choices are further dictated by distress severity and depth: Partial-Depth Repair addresses shallow surface defects confined to the upper slab portion, whereas Full-Depth Pavement Repair is required when damage penetrates deeper than one-third of the slab thickness or compromises structural integrity. [4][5][9] Additionally, Grout Injection for Slab Stabilization is selectively applied to structurally sound slabs exhibiting only localized settlement without extensive cracking or faulting. [1][2]

Condition assessment and timing

Condition assessment and timing ensure preservation treatments are applied only when the pavement remains in relatively good condition with ample remaining service life, typically indicated by a Pavement Condition Rating between 60 and 100. [1][6] Accurate evaluation of distress severity—ranging from shallow surface defects suitable for Partial-Depth Repair (PDR) or Grout Injection to deep structural failures requiring Full-Depth Pavement Repair—is required to select the appropriate intervention before deterioration accelerates beyond the point where localized corrective actions can be effective. [1][5][6]

Materials and repair details

Interventions are supported by Grout Injection for Slab Stabilization, which corrects localized settlement and voids without altering surface geometry. [8] Together, these materials and details function to arrest early-stage deterioration, prevent water infiltration, and maintain ride quality before more invasive rehabilitation becomes necessary. [3]

Treatment procedures

Concrete pavement preservation employs a tiered strategy of localized interventions designed to arrest early-stage deterioration and extend service life without increasing structural capacity. [3][6] For shallow defects confined to the upper portion of the slab, Partial-Depth Repair techniques remove unsound material and replace it with compatible cementitious mixes to restore ride quality and prevent water infiltration. [4][5] When distress penetrates deeper into the structure or compromises load transfer, Full-Depth Pavement Repair restores structural integrity by replacing the damaged concrete through the entire slab thickness, while Grout Injection addresses localized settlement by lifting settled slabs back to grade. [3][5][8]
